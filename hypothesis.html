§ 01 · Core Proposition

The Pasigraphic
Instruction Engine

The Voynich Manuscript (Beinecke MS 408, c. 1404–1438) is not a phonetic cipher of any natural language, nor a hoax, nor automatic writing. It is a Pasigraphic Instruction Engine — a constructed, script-independent notation system designed to encode Renaissance-era spagyric (alchemical-pharmaceutical) laboratory procedures in a language-independent format.

Its vocabulary does not represent sounds. The text demonstrates properties of a combinatorial instruction engine (an Abstract Syntax Tree) where words act as modular chemical/pharmaceutical formulas: [Space] + [Action] + [Substance] + [Yield]. Every token encodes an ontological category, making the text a procedural database rather than a narrative. The illustrations serve as the structural index anchoring each passage to its physical referent.

This hypothesis builds on prior computational work (Earnhart 2025 "Paper Computer" model; Layfield & Davis 2026 LSA study) while extending it across over 100 independent detailed analyses using NLP, statistical mechanics, topological clustering, pixel-level spatial analysis, and isomorphic translation against 35 historical corpora from the 1300–1600 AD period.

PILLAR 4 · PHASES 7–9, 27–29

Different manuscript sections (Herbal, Astronomy, Balneology, Recipes) use fundamentally different root vocabularies. The Chaos Baseline & Rebuttal of Auto-Copying

A common skeptical argument (e.g., the Timm & Schinner model) posits that the Voynich text is an "auto-copying" artifact, where the author subconsciously repeated previous character strings, artificially lowering entropy. To test this definitively, we generated a 15,000-word synthetic control group mimicking the proposed auto-copying mechanism, alongside a strict Chaos Baseline (100–1,000 iterations of completely shuffled Voynich text).

The results were unequivocal: the syntactic rigidity and morphological valency of the actual manuscript survived with a Z-Score > 5.0 against the Chaos Baseline. The auto-copying control group produced an entropy spike approaching 14.19, utterly failing to replicate the tight ~4.44 perplexity of the genuine MS. The structure is deliberate and engineered, not an artifact of random human repetition or self-citation.

"The Voynich Manuscript achieves low entropy (high predictability) combined with extreme combinatorial valency — a combination that is structurally impossible for any known natural language and mathematically strongly suggests a synthetic, apriori taxonomic architecture."

Multimodal Grammar: Topographical Color Coding

The abstract syntax tree (AST) structure is only half the equation. Our visual audits revealed that the manuscript's author used colors not for artistic decoration, but as thermodynamic macro-commands tightly bound to the text syntax. By integrating color into the compiler's logical tree, massive ambiguities in the procedural instructions were resolved:

  • Red (Sulphur/Fire): Active heating, roasting, or pressure. Visually correlates with textual heat operations (e.g., ch-).
  • Blue/White (Mercury/Water): Washing, cooling, and condensation. Mathematically aligns with textual roots for liquid actions (e.g., so-, sa-).
  • Yellow (Sal/Citrinitas): Slow curing in moderate heat, solid salt extraction.
  • Green (Viriditas): Raw, unprocessed materia prima and plant saps.
  • Unpainted Space: Ether, vapors, and aromatic exhalations.

This multimodal integration means the text cannot be read in isolation. The visual illustrations provide the functional arguments that the syntactic formulas operate on.

Balneology as Hydraulic Engineering

The traditional assumption posits that the women bathing in pools depict health rituals or anatomical treatises. However, viewing these folios from an engineering or hydraulic perspective reveals strong parallels with early engineering texts (such as those by Al-Jazari or Agostino Ramelli). In these traditions, figures of women or animals were commonly used to represent "automata" — effectively functioning as fluid level indicators, valves, or flow directors. The tubes and clouds seen throughout the Voynich illustrations visually match closed distillation, condensation, and reflux systems (like Pelican flasks). This interpretation mathematically aligns with a distinct drop in manual operational commands within this section of the text, as the processes depicted are automated via hydraulic circulation.

Visual Camouflage: Ethiopian Magic & Astromedicine

If the cosmological and balneological sections describe industrial distillation and hydraulic engineering, why do they look like horoscopes with faces inside stars or nymphs in green pools? Our analysis (Phases 129–132) reveals this as a deliberate protection mechanism against the Inquisition.

The author used the visual templates of Coptic/Ethiopian Orthodox magic (Solomon's Net) and Arabic Astromedicine (Picatrix) as a "shell." To the casual observer or a 15th-century inquisitor, the manuscript appears to be a standard astrological or magical amulet. Consequently, modern attempts to literally interpret the drawings as metal pipes or screws are misguided. The engineering exists, but it is encoded through the aesthetic lens of mystical allegory and thermodynamic color macros.

Visual Dose Validation & The Anti-Gematria Proof

If the manuscript were a simple phonetic cipher or a direct picture-code (gematria), every botanical illustration would mathematically match the frequency of its associated text labels. To test this hypothesis, we deployed a custom OpenCV algorithm across all 130 botanical pages. The result was a global mathematical correlation of r < 0.3.

Paradoxically, this lack of global correlation is precisely what proves the Pasigraphic Instruction Engine. The text is a procedural recipe, not a simple visual caption. The numbers align only where the author needed to depict a specific formulaic proportion. Elsewhere, the author drew a generic bush for field identification, while the recipe required much smaller doses. § 06 · Competing Theories

Evaluation of
Alternative Hypotheses

The data generated across all phases provides a clear mathematical basis for evaluating every major competing hypothesis. The following verdicts are based on specific, quantified evidence — not on subjective assessment.

The Hoax / Card-and-Grille Theory (Rugg 2004)

The manuscript was generated mechanically using a Cardan grille, producing meaningless but statistically plausible text. Refuted by: Visual-Semantic Alignment (Phase 22) — 43.1% match rate between fluid-related tokens and drawn water/vat illustrations is structurally impossible for a blind generator. Sectoral Polymorphism (Phase 29) MCC = 0.40 on unseen quires; all Markov and repetitive-formula controls collapse to MCC ≈ 0.00 under identical conditions.

REFUTED

Glossolalia / Trance Writing

The author suffered from a dissociative condition and produced meaningless automatic writing. Refuted by: Dependency Graph Asymmetry (Phase 15) — trance writing produces bidirectional ~50/50 flow; the manuscript shows a deliberate ~65/35 Forward/Backward ratio. Spatial Polymorphism (Phase 18) — the text dynamically alters formatting rules (indentation, concentric orbits) based on the specific drawn subject. Unconscious writing cannot execute 100% deterministic layout shifts in response to illustration type.

REFUTED

Standard Substitution Cipher (Vigenère / ROT-N)

A standard polyalphabetic or simple substitution cipher of a natural European language. Refuted by: Synthetic Gauntlet (Phase 25) — a Vigenère cipher applied to Latin plaintext causes perplexity to spike to 6.17 and valency to collapse to 0.00. The authentic Voynich remains at ~4.46 / ~33. Morphological valency ~86 in the full corpus is structurally incompatible with any substitution-based encoding.

REFUTED

Verbose / Naibbe Cipher (Greshko)

The text encodes a short plaintext skeleton padded with large amounts of meaningless "null" characters. Refuted by: ZLIB compression ratio ~0.31 is entirely normal for specialized historical technical text (between English Theology ~0.26 and German Botany ~0.33). Signal-to-Noise ratio is 80% signal / 20% syntax — the inverse of a verbose cipher. In a true verbose cipher, padding dominates; here it does not.

REFUTED

Artificial Philosophical Language (Wilkins / Dalgarno precedent)

The manuscript is an a priori philosophical language where tokens represent ontological positions rather than phonetic sounds. Supported and refined: The morphological architecture (BPE valency ~86), the taxonomic lexeme extraction (Phase 12), and the prefix-driven sectoral classification all align with this theory. The refinement: the Voynich system is not a general-purpose language — it is domain-specific to 15th-century alchemical-botanical procedure, predating Wilkins by ~200 years.

SUPPORTED

Markov / Finite-State Automaton Model (Earnhart 2025)

The manuscript was generated by a highly constrained deterministic Markov process / Paper Computer. Supported with refinement: Phase 30 V2 grammar induction confirms flat syntax (1.9% parse coverage at depth 3 — identical to a 3-gram Markov chain). Second-order entropy ~0.14 bits confirms near-determinism. However, a blind Markov chain cannot reproduce the spatial and sectoral polymorphism — the author used Markov-like local syntax constraints within a globally deterministic spatial structure.

REFINED
§ 07 · Epistemological Limits

What We Know
and What We Don't

Scientific integrity requires an explicit separation of empirical structural findings from interpretive semantic hypotheses. The following audit outlines the boundaries of current knowledge.

Empirical Findings:
The physical layout of the text is highly correlated with the illustrations (Edge Bias). The manuscript's morphological structure (low entropy, extreme valency) differs substantially from known natural languages and standard substitution ciphers. The vocabulary shifts systematically across thematic sections. The text obeys a deterministic, feed-forward syntactic chain structure.

⚠ Interpretive Limitations

  • No semantic proof: Machine Learning approaches (like MLM predicting missing tokens) demonstrate strict structural syntax, not semantic meaning.
  • No phonetic values: All etymological mappings are interpretive hypotheses, not demonstrated translations.
  • Historical context: The identification of pipes and fluids as alchemical must acknowledge the period's dominant humoral theory (fluids and vessels often represented medical or biological processes).
  • Phase 23 Direct Translation was formally retracted. Nearest-neighbour results in unaligned embeddings are statistically unreliable as direct translations.

What Would Falsify This Hypothesis?

This hypothesis is empirically testable and would be significantly weakened if:

  • A natural language (or a simple phonetic cipher thereof) is found that produces an identical statistical footprint (Perplexity ~4.4, Valency ~86).
  • It is demonstrated mathematically that the "valency" metric is an artefact of transcription choice rather than an intrinsic property of the text.
  • A simple mechanical generative model is built that reproduces both the text's local properties and its macro-level spatial/domain correlations simultaneously.

This study has mapped the topology and layout geometry of the manuscript. What it encodes — the specific identity of its objects, the exact meaning of its symbols — remains an open question requiring methodologies beyond structural computational linguistics.

§ 08 · Linguistic Substrate (Phases 127–130)

The Latin Substrate
and the Materia Medica

Phases 127–130 represent a significant advancement in the decoding effort. For the first time, systematic root extraction and language affinity mapping provide quantitative evidence for the manuscript's linguistic substrate.

Key Discovery (Phase 127): Universal root extraction across all three transcriptions yielded 2,263 unique roots at 100% cross-transcription consensus. Language affinity testing against 13 historical corpora produced 408,465 phonetic matches — with Latin accounting for 91.52% (373,839 matches) in this test setup. Arabic produced no direct matches. This provides strong empirical grounds to doubt the Arabic origin hypothesis, and points to a Latin substrate.

Synthesis of East and West: The text exhibits a distinct duality. The phonetic foundation of the roots aligns strongly with Western Latin medical terminology (e.g., kal for salts/lime, tal for metals, ol for oils) serving as a lingua franca. However, the syntax and structuring methodology echo Eastern and Islamic traditions. The morphology resembles Ibn Arabi's "Science of Letters" (ʿilm al-ḥurūf), where letter forms hold procedural power. The text's overarching architecture employs a talismanic framing similar to Fatimid or Mamluk scrolls: it uses initiators (like sol-, sho- acting as a Basmala-like opening), procedure commands, and terminators (like -dy, -am acting as an Amen-like close). Furthermore, the "Gallows" characters appear to function not as phonetic letters but as non-phonetic operational symbols (Charaktêres) from Greco-Egyptian and Jewish magic traditions, indicating specific physical laboratory actions.
