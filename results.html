Limitations & Alternative Explanations

Crucial methodological caveats for evaluating the machine learning and structural data.

Syntax vs. Semantics in ML

Masked Language Modeling (MLM), Word2Vec, and SVD clustering successfully extract patterns and predict missing tokens. However, it must be explicitly stated: this only demonstrates strict structural syntax. It does not mathematically prove semantic meaning. A model trained on structured but meaningless text (e.g., generated Markov chains) will also "learn" rules. The topological findings here are evidence of a robust formal system, not direct translations.

BPE Valency as a Hypothesis

The extremely high Morphological Valency (~86.5) indicates the text is extraordinarily compact. While this strongly suggests an algorithmic or taxonomic system, it is also a recognized signature of very specific historical cipher systems (like complex nomenclators) or highly synthetic constructed languages. It remains a supporting hypothesis, not absolute proof of pasigraphy.

Historical Context: Humoral Theory

The interpretation of "plumbing", "vats", and "fluid flows" as alchemical or spagyric must be balanced against the dominant medical paradigm of the 15th century: Humoral Theory. The visual depiction of connected vessels could equally represent blood, bile, phlegm, and physiological processes without any hidden "chemical code." The pasigraphic hypothesis is presented as the most topologically consistent model, but biological-humoral interpretations remain entirely valid.

Alternative Null Hypotheses Needed

Future work must move beyond comparing Voynichese to "random noise." The next critical baseline test involves testing the VMS metrics against known historical structural ciphers (e.g., Cardan grilles, Naibbe ciphers, homophonic substitution) to see if these systems can artificially replicate the 1.8x edge bias and ~4.4 perplexity simultaneously.
